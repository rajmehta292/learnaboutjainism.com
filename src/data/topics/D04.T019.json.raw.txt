  "overview": "In Jain philosophical literature, \"Jiva-kanda\" (or \"Jivakanda\") refers to the systematic treatment of the soul (jiva) and its classifications, most prominently in the Digambara text Gommatsara Jiva-kanda by Acharya Nemichandra Siddhanta Chakravarti. This portion of Gommatsara belongs to the karmic-technical branch of Jain scriptural analysis (karananuyoga) and aims to describe, with great precision, the varieties, conditions, and trajectories of souls bound in samsara as well as their path to liberation. [[0]](https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/gommatsara-by-acharya-nemichandra/d/doc1679458.html)\n\nThe Jiva-kanda material organizes its discussion through a series of prakaranas (chapters) that move from broad spiritual stages to increasingly fine-grained classifications. First, it outlines the fourteen gunasthanas (spiritual stages), beginning with complete delusion (mithyatva) and culminating in omniscience (kevala-jnana) and final liberation (moksha). These stages are defined by the changing relationship between the soul and karmic matter—whether karma is being accumulated, suppressed, or destroyed—and they provide the overarching framework within which all other classifications are interpreted. [[1]](https://jaingpt.org/knowledge/gommatasara_jiva_kanda_001612_001612) [[0]](https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/gommatsara-by-acharya-nemichandra/d/doc1679458.html)\n\nWithin this framework, Jiva-kanda presents detailed \"soul-classes\" (jiva-samasa). Souls are classified by senses (from one-sensed to five-sensed), mobility (immobile/sthavar vs mobile/tras), embodiment (earth-bodied, water-bodied, fire-bodied, air-bodied, plant-bodied, and various animal and human forms), development capacity (paryapti, or the ability to fully develop bodily and sensory faculties), sexuality (veda), passion states (kashaya), knowledge types (jnana), control or restraint (samyama), thought-paints (leshya), liberability (bhavyatva vs abhavyatva), rationality (sanjnitva), and assimilation (ahara). The text famously enumerates 406, and more precisely 98, classes of souls by combining these criteria, showing how every living being in the cosmos can be located within a rigorous classificatory grid. [[1]](https://jaingpt.org/knowledge/gommatasara_jiva_kanda_001612_001612) [[0]](https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/gommatsara-by-acharya-nemichandra/d/doc1679458.html)\n\nA central set of classifications concerns the senses and mobility of samsari (worldly) jivas. Immobile, single-sensed beings (sthavar) are further divided into earth-bodied (prithvikaya), water-bodied (apkaya), fire-bodied (tejakaya), air-bodied (vayukaya), and plant-bodied (vanaspatikaya) souls. Plant-bodied souls themselves are subdivided into individual (pratyeka) and common or group-souled (sadharana, including nigoda beings). Mobile beings (tras) possess two to five senses, culminating in five-sensed beings with mind, such as humans, many animals, and celestial and infernal beings. These classifications emphasize that even the most subtle or seemingly inert forms of existence are inhabited by conscious jivas endowed with vitalities (pranas). [[2]](https://iaspoint.com/jiva-and-ajiva/) [[3]](https://jainknowledge.com/question/9217/explain-the-categorisation-of-jivas)\n\nJiva-kanda also devotes chapters to paryapti (developableness) and prana (vitalities). Paryapti describes the processes by which a soul, upon embodiment, completes the development of its body, senses, respiration, speech, and mind. Prana refers to the ten vitalities—five senses, mind, speech, body-power, respiration, and age—that mark a living being. Consciousness (chetana) and its active manifestation (upayoga) remain the defining features of jiva, but these vitalities and developmental capacities explain how consciousness is expressed through particular bodies and life-forms. [[1]](https://jaingpt.org/knowledge/gommatasara_jiva_kanda_001612_001612) [[4]](https://jaingpt.org/knowledge/samyag_gyan_charitra_01_010074_010074)\n\nAnother major classificatory scheme in Jiva-kanda is the set of fourteen \"soul-quests\" (marganas), which provide different lenses for identifying and analyzing souls: conditions of existence (gati), senses (indriya), embodiments (kaya), vibrations (yoga), sex inclinations (veda), passions (kashaya), knowledge (jnana), control (samyama), conation (darshana), thought-paints (leshya), liberability (bhavyatva), right-belief (samyaktva), rationality (sanjnitva), and assimilation (ahara). These marganas are then correlated with the fourteen gunasthanas in an appendix, showing how each soul-quest changes as the soul advances spiritually. The result is a multi-dimensional classification system in which every jiva can be studied from the standpoint of its karmic condition, embodiment, psychological tendencies, and spiritual potential. [[1]](https://jaingpt.org/knowledge/gommatasara_jiva_kanda_001612_001612) [[0]](https://www.wisdomlib.org/jainism/book/gommatsara-by-acharya-nemichandra/d/doc1679458.html)\n\nIn summary, \"Jiva-kanda classifications\" refer not to a single list but to an interconnected network of schemes—senses and mobility, embodiment, vitalities, development, passions, knowledge, rationality, liberability, and spiritual stages—through which Jain thinkers, especially in the Digambara tradition, have sought to map the entire living universe. These classifications are descriptive rather than prescriptive: they do not assign moral worth to particular life-forms but reveal the complexity of samsara and the many conditions under which the same fundamentally pure jiva can wander or progress toward liberation. Modern readers often encounter these systems through translations and commentaries, which aim to clarify technical terms while preserving the careful, layered structure of the original Jiva-kanda material.
