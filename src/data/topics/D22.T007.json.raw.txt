  "overview": "Kalyanamandira Stotra (often written as Kalyan Mandir Stotra) is a celebrated Jain hymn in praise of Tirthankara Parshvanatha, the twenty‑third Tirthankara. It is widely recited in both Śvetāmbara and Digambara communities as a devotional text emphasizing the protective, liberative, and auspicious qualities of the Jina’s feet, metaphorically described as a “temple of welfare” (kalyāṇa‑mandira). The stotra presents Parshvanatha as the refuge for beings drowning in saṃsāra, the destroyer of sins, and the source of fearlessness for those afflicted by worldly suffering. [[0]](https://jaindivya.com/kalyan-mandir-stotra-complete-transliteration-with-meaning/) [[1]](https://encyclopediaofjainism.com/kalyan-mandir-stotra/)\n\nThe hymn is composed in refined Sanskrit, drawing on classical poetic imagery—oceans, jewels, comets, lotuses, cool breezes—to express the inexpressible greatness of the Tirthankara. Early verses explicitly acknowledge the poet’s inadequacy to fully describe the Jina’s qualities, comparing this task to an owl’s chick trying to describe the blazing sun. The text thus combines humility, intense devotion, and doctrinal themes: the destruction of karma, the cooling of passions, and the guidance of souls across the ocean of rebirth. [[0]](https://jaindivya.com/kalyan-mandir-stotra-complete-transliteration-with-meaning/) [[1]](https://encyclopediaofjainism.com/kalyan-mandir-stotra/)\n\nWithin Jain practice, Kalyanamandira Stotra is often recited in daily worship, special pūjās, and protective rites. Many community editions pair the Sanskrit text with vernacular explanations in Hindi, Gujarati, or other languages, making its imagery accessible to lay practitioners. Commentarial traditions highlight how the stotra can be understood as a meditation aid: by contemplating the qualities of Parshvanatha described in the verses, the devotee is encouraged to cultivate faith (śraddhā), detachment, and right view, while remembering that liberation ultimately depends on one’s own effort guided by the Jina’s teaching. [[2]](https://encyclopediaofjainism.com/%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%B2%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A3-%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%B0-%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0/) [[3]](https://jainqq.org/booktext/Kalyan_Mandir_Stotra/009418)\n\nThere is some disagreement in the tradition regarding authorship. Several modern devotional presentations attribute the stotra to Ācārya Kumudacandra, a Jain teacher known for hymns to Parshvanatha, while other manuscript‑based and scholarly sources identify Ācārya Siddhasena Divākara (also called Siddhasena Sūri) as the original author, situating the text in a broader historical narrative of his life and missionary work. Because of this, responsible study of the stotra notes the uncertainty and treats both attributions with care, pending detailed manuscript research and lineage‑based confirmation.
