It was founded in 1970 under the inspiration and leadership of the ninth Terāpanth Ācārya, Gurudev Acharya Tulsi, as a modern institutional framework to research, teach, and disseminate Jain philosophy, ethics, and contemplative practices in dialogue with contemporary society. [[0]](https://vvvschool.com/AboutUs/About%20JVB/) JVB functions as the sponsoring body for the Jain Vishva Bharati Institute (JVBI), a deemed university recognized under Section 3 of the University Grants Commission Act, 1956, which was notified in 1991 by the Government of India. [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_Vishva_Bharati_Institute) [[2]](https://jvbi.ac.in/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=758)\n\nFrom its inception, Jain Vishva Bharati has articulated a mission grounded in classical Jain ideals—particularly anekānta (non‑one‑sidedness), ahiṃsā (non‑violence), tolerance, and peaceful coexistence—while adopting modern institutional forms such as universities, research centers, and structured curricula. The preamble of JVBI’s constitution explicitly states that the Institute is an endeavour to put into practice, promote, and propagate these ideals for the welfare of humanity, and to establish a university of advanced studies, research, and training in Jainology in the context of comparative Indology, world religions, non‑violence, and world peace. [[2]](https://jvbi.ac.in/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=758) [[3]](https://jvbi.ac.in/dde/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=758) This dual orientation—rooted in Terāpanth spiritual lineage yet open to global academic and ethical discourse—defines JVB’s distinctive place in modern Jain history.\n\nOrganizationally, JVB and JVBI share a common campus at Ladnun, which is often described as an oasis‑like environment in the arid zone of Rajasthan, with greenery, parks, and a consciously cultivated spiritual ambience reminiscent of the traditional gurukula model of learning. [[2]](https://jvbi.ac.in/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=758) [[3]](https://jvbi.ac.in/dde/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=758) The campus hosts educational facilities, residential quarters, meditation centers, and libraries, as well as spaces for community service and cultural activities. JVB’s activities are typically grouped into seven broad areas: education, research, literature, meditation, community services, cultural progression, and coordination. [[0]](https://vvvschool.com/AboutUs/About%20JVB/) These domains reflect the Terāpanth emphasis on integrating scriptural study (jñāna), contemplative practice (dhyāna), and ethical conduct (ācāra) within a structured institutional framework.\n\nThe spiritual leadership of Jain Vishva Bharati is closely tied to the Terāpanth ācāryas. Acharya Tulsi served as the founding inspiration and first constitutional anūśāsaka (moral and spiritual guide) of JVBI, followed by Acharya Mahapragya as the second anūśāsaka, and Acharya Mahashraman as the present spiritual head. [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_Vishva_Bharati_Institute) [[2]](https://jvbi.ac.in/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=146&Itemid=758) [[4]](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/6147398) The role of the anūśāsaka is statutorily defined: to provide spiritual and moral direction, ensure that academic and administrative decisions remain aligned with Jain ethical principles, and safeguard the non‑sectarian, peace‑oriented ethos of the institution. While the leadership is rooted in the Terāpanth lineage, the Institute’s stated orientation is to transcend narrow sectarianism and engage with broader philosophical and religious traditions.\n\nAcademically, Jain Vishva Bharati Institute offers a wide range of programs from undergraduate degrees to doctoral and post‑doctoral research, with particular strengths in Jainology and Comparative Religion & Philosophy, Prakrit and Jain Āgama studies, Sanskrit, Yoga and Science of Living, Non‑violence and Peace, Social Work, Education, and related disciplines. [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_Vishva_Bharati_Institute) [[4]](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/6147398) The curriculum in Jainology typically covers Jain metaphysics, ethics, meditation, karma theory, epistemology, logic, and anekānta, often in comparative dialogue with other Indian philosophical schools, Western philosophy, and world religions. Courses in Prakrit and Jain Āgama focus on canonical texts, linguistic skills, and textual hermeneutics, thereby linking modern students to primary scriptural sources.\n\nBeyond formal degrees, JVB and JVBI run certificate and diploma programs in areas such as Preksha meditation, yoga, non‑violence and peace, journalism and mass media, office automation, and web design, as well as distance education offerings that extend Jain value‑based education to a wider audience. [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_Vishva_Bharati_Institute) Preksha meditation—systematized within the Terāpanth tradition by Acharya Mahapragya—is a central contemplative practice promoted on campus, with an international meditation center that emphasizes holistic well‑being, ethical self‑discipline, and spiritual introspection. [[0]](https://vvvschool.com/AboutUs/About%20JVB/) These initiatives reflect JVB’s attempt to translate classical Jain practices into accessible, structured programs for contemporary seekers and students.\n\nIn the broader landscape of Jain institutions, Jain Vishva Bharati occupies a significant position as a bridge between monastic leadership and lay education, and between traditional scriptural authority and modern academic standards. It has contributed to the systematization of Jain studies as an interdisciplinary field, combining philology, philosophy, ethics, and peace studies. Its publications and research projects have focused on editing and interpreting Jain canonical and post‑canonical texts, exploring the practical implications of ahiṃsā and anekānta in modern conflicts, and developing pedagogies for value education in schools and universities. [[0]](https://vvvschool.com/AboutUs/About%20JVB/) [[4]](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/6147398) At the same time, JVB remains embedded in the lived practice of the Terāpanth community, supporting spiritual camps, youth programs, and community service initiatives that embody Jain ethical ideals in everyday life.\n\nHistorically, the emergence of Jain Vishva Bharati in 1970 and the recognition of JVBI as a deemed university in 1991 can be seen as part of a wider movement in twentieth‑century Jainism to institutionalize religious education, respond to globalization, and engage with modern concerns such as peace, human rights, and environmental ethics. The Terāpanth leadership, especially Acharya Tulsi and Acharya Mahapragya, envisioned JVB as a ‘kāmadhenu’—a wish‑fulfilling cow—for the community, capable of generating scholarship, spiritual training, and social service in a sustained manner. [[0]](https://vvvschool.com/AboutUs/About%20JVB/) In contemporary times, under Acharya Mahashraman’s guidance, JVB continues to expand its reach, including international collaborations and outreach programs, while maintaining its core commitment to Jain ethical and contemplative traditions.\n\nFrom a Jain studies perspective, Jain Vishva Bharati is thus best understood not merely as a single institution, but as a complex ecosystem: a sponsoring body, a deemed university, a research and publication center, a meditation hub, and a community service platform, all oriented around the Terāpanth interpretation of Jain dharma. Its significance lies in how it operationalizes classical Jain ideals—ahiṃsā, anekānta, aparigraha (non‑possessiveness), and self‑discipline—within modern educational and social structures, offering a living case study of the interaction between tradition and modernity in contemporary Jainism.",
