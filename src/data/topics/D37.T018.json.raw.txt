  "overview": "Airavata Kshetra (Sanskrit/Prakrit: Airāvatakṣetra, often rendered in Hindi as ऐरावत क्षेत्र) is a cosmological region described in Jain sacred geography within Jambūdvīpa, the central continent of the Jain universe. It is presented in the canonical and post‑canonical cosmology texts as one of the major kṣetras (regions) structurally parallel to Bharata Kshetra, but located to the north of key mountains and to the south of the outer ring of Jambūdvīpa. In the layered depiction of the universe, Airavata Kshetra belongs to the human-inhabited zones where spiritual practice, the appearance of Tirthankaras, and the cycles of time (kāla) unfold.\n\nJain cosmological works describe Airavata Kshetra as lying between significant mountains and seas, with its own system of rivers, peaks, and sub‑regions. It is associated with the mountain Vijayārddha (Vijayardha/Vijayārddha Parvata), upon which nine notable peaks or \"kūṭas\" are named: Siddha, Airāvata, Khaṇḍaprapāta, Māṇibhadra, Vijayārddha, Pūrṇabhadra, Timiśraguhā, Airāvata (a second peak of the same name), and Vaiśravaṇa. These features mirror, in a structurally symmetric way, the arrangement of mountains and rivers in Bharata Kshetra, underscoring the Jain idea that the universe is ordered and balanced across multiple regions. [[0]](https://encyclopediaofjainism.com/%E0%A4%90%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%A4-%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0-%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE-%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%A8/)\n\nThe rivers of Airavata Kshetra are described in detail. From the Mahāpuṇḍarīka lake on the summit of Śikhari (Śikhari Parvata), the river Raktā flows out of the eastern gate and falls into the Raktakuṇḍa, then continues to the Lavana-samudra (Salt Ocean). From the western gate, the river Raktodā emerges, falls into the Raktodakuṇḍa, and then flows westward, joining other rivers before entering the Lavana-samudra through the island’s rim. Within Airavata Kshetra, the side facing the ocean is called the Āryakhaṇḍa (Arya region), while five other divisions are termed Mleccha‑khaṇḍas (non‑Arya regions). In the central Mleccha‑khaṇḍa stands Vṛṣabha Parvata, upon which universal monarchs (cakravartins) inscribe their praises, echoing similar motifs found in Bharata Kshetra. [[0]](https://encyclopediaofjainism.com/%E0%A4%90%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%A4-%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0-%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE-%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%A8/)\n\nCosmology texts emphasize that in the Āryakhaṇḍa of Airavata Kshetra, the sixfold cycles of time—ascending (utsarpiṇī) and descending (avasarpiṇī), each divided into six eras—occur in the same pattern as in Bharata’s Āryakhaṇḍa. This parallelism supports the doctrinal claim that multiple regions of Jambūdvīpa host human civilizations, spiritual teachers, and opportunities for liberation, not just the specific historical geography of India. The rivers Ganga, Rohita, Harita, Sītā, Nārī, Suvarṇakūlā, and Raktā are said to flow eastward, while Sindhu, Rohitāsyā, Harikāntā, Sītodā, Narakāntā, Rūpyakūlā, and Raktodā flow westward into the western ocean, further elaborating the sacred map of Airavata Kshetra. [[0]](https://encyclopediaofjainism.com/%E0%A4%90%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%A4-%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0-%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE-%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%A8/)\n\nIn narrative literature such as Hemacandra’s \"Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra\" (Lives of the Sixty‑Three Illustrious Persons), Airavatakshetra is mentioned as the region in which the city Śatadvāra (Śatadvara) is located and as the birthplace of certain Tirthankaras. This connects the abstract cosmological description with the biographical cycles of the Jinas, showing that the appearance of enlightened beings is not confined to one historical land but distributed across multiple kṣetras in Jambūdvīpa. [[1]](https://www.wisdomlib.org/concept/airavatakshetra)\n\nTraditional cosmology manuals such as \"Trilokabhāskara\" and related Jain encyclopedic works present Airavata Kshetra alongside other regions—Bharata, Haimavata, Hari, Videha, Devakuru, Uttarakuru, Ramyaka, Hairaṇyavata, etc.—to illustrate the symmetry of the universe. They stress that the arrangement of mountains, lakes, rivers, and time cycles in Airavata Kshetra’s Āryakhaṇḍa is analogous to that of Bharata’s Āryakhaṇḍa, reinforcing the doctrinal idea that dharma, spiritual progress, and the possibility of mokṣa are cosmologically widespread. At the same time, these texts acknowledge that our current human experience is limited to Bharata Kshetra, and knowledge of Airavata Kshetra is mediated through scriptural revelation rather than empirical travel.\n\nIn contemporary Jain education, Airavata Kshetra is taught primarily as a part of cosmology rather than as a physical pilgrimage destination. It functions as a conceptual reminder that Jainism envisions a vast, ordered universe populated by many regions where beings undergo karmic cycles and can attain liberation. The detailed descriptions of Airavata Kshetra’s geography, rivers, and mountains serve both as symbolic teaching tools and as a structured cosmological map, inviting reflection on the scale of samsāra and the universality of dharma across space and time.",
