  "overview": "Chandraprajnapti (also spelled Chandra-pragnapti, Chandapannatti) is a canonical Jain text belonging to the group of Upanga Agamas in the Śvetāmbara tradition. It is usually counted as the sixth Upanga and is closely paired with its sister text, Sūryaprajnapti, which treats the sun; Chandraprajnapti focuses on the moon, lunar motions, and their role within Jain cosmology. Together, these texts form an important part of the Jain canonical corpus concerned with astronomy, cosmography, and the measurement of time.\n\nThe work is composed in Ardhamāgadhī Prakrit and survives in several recensions transmitted within different Śvetāmbara sub-traditions, including Mūrtipūjaka, Sthānakavāsī, and Terāpanth lineages. Traditional accounts attribute the doctrinal content of Chandraprajnapti to Mahāvīra’s teachings, preserved by his gaṇadharas and later redacted in the canonical collections. As with other Upanga texts, modern scholarship treats the attribution as doctrinal rather than strictly historical, but the text is still regarded as part of the authoritative Śvetāmbara canon.\n\nDoctrinally, Chandraprajnapti describes the nature of the moon (candra), its dimensions, its position in the Jain universe, and its cyclical phases. It explains how the waxing and waning of the moon relate to Jain calendrical reckoning, auspicious and inauspicious times, and the structuring of religious observances. The text situates the moon within the three worlds of Jain cosmology, detailing its relation to Mount Meru, the continents and oceans, and the heavenly abodes. In doing so, it reflects the broader Jain project of mapping spiritual practice onto a highly structured cosmic order.\n\nThe text also contains lists, numerical descriptions, and classifications that show an early Jain engagement with systematic astronomy. While its cosmological model differs from modern scientific astronomy, Chandraprajnapti demonstrates careful observation of lunar cycles and an interest in regularity, calculation, and prediction. Commentarial traditions—both classical and modern—often highlight this aspect, presenting the text as an example of Jain intellectual engagement with the natural world.\n\nWithin practice, Chandraprajnapti is not typically used as a direct manual for lay ritual, but it underlies the calendrical frameworks that guide fasting days, festival dates, and other observances. Monastic and scholarly communities study it as part of the broader Agamic curriculum, often alongside Sūryaprajnapti and cosmological texts such as Jambūdvīpaprajnapti. In contemporary Jain education, Chandraprajnapti is sometimes introduced in advanced courses on Jain cosmology and scriptural studies, where its technical descriptions are interpreted symbolically as reminders of order, interdependence, and the vastness of saṃsāra.\n\nModern editions and translations of Chandraprajnapti frequently include extensive introductions, diagrams of the Jain universe, and comparative notes with other cosmological texts. These materials help readers understand both the literal content and the interpretive possibilities of the text. Some scholars read Chandraprajnapti primarily as a historical document of Jain cosmological thought, while traditional commentators emphasize its role in preserving the omniscient perspective of the Tīrthaṅkaras on the structure of the universe. Both approaches agree that Chandraprajnapti is a key witness to how Jainism integrates cosmology, time-reckoning, and spiritual discipline into a coherent worldview.",
