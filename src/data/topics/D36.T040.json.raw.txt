  "overview": "Paul Dundas (1952–2023) was a British Indologist and one of the most influential modern scholars of Jainism and Prakrit studies. Trained in Sanskrit and Middle Indo-Aryan philology, he spent the bulk of his academic career at the University of Edinburgh, where he served as senior lecturer in Sanskrit and later as Head of Asian Studies, eventually becoming an honorary fellow in Sanskrit. His work combined close reading of primary texts with historical and sociological sensitivity, making him a central figure in the global academic study of Jain traditions.\n\nDundas’s most widely known book, \"The Jains\" (first published in 1992 and revised in 2002), quickly became—and remains—one of the standard English-language introductions to Jainism. In this volume he presents the history, doctrines, sectarian developments, ritual practices, and social contexts of Jain communities, drawing on both Śvetāmbara and Digambara sources. The book is notable for its careful attention to non-violence (ahiṃsā), anekāntavāda (the doctrine of non-one-sidedness), karma theory, and the lived realities of Jain laypeople and ascetics, while consistently distinguishing descriptive scholarship from internal theological claims.  [[0]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dundas) [[1]](https://www.soas.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-02/Cort%20IJJS%2020%2C%201%202024%20Print%201%20%283%29_0.pdf)\n\nBeyond this synthetic introduction, Dundas produced specialized studies that reshaped understanding of Jain history and sectarianism. His monograph \"History, Scripture and Controversy in a Medieval Jain Sect\" (2007) offers a detailed examination of medieval Śvetāmbara sectarian debates, scriptural authority, and the dynamics of reform and continuity. Earlier, he had worked on the Sattasaī and its commentators, contributing to the study of Prakrit literature, and he wrote influential articles on topics such as the nature of the kevalin (omniscient being), the Digambara Jain warrior, and the so‑called \"dark age\" of Jainism between major imperial formations.  [[1]](https://www.soas.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-02/Cort%20IJJS%2020%2C%201%202024%20Print%201%20%283%29_0.pdf) [[2]](https://en.bharatpedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dundas)\n\nDundas also engaged deeply with broader Sanskrit literary culture. His edition and translation of Māgha’s \"Śiśupālavadha\" (\"The Killing of Shishupala\") for the Murty Classical Library of India (2017) demonstrated his mastery of classical Sanskrit poetics and his commitment to making complex texts accessible to modern readers. He contributed numerous encyclopedia entries, book chapters, and articles on major Jain authors such as Haribhadra, Hemacandra, Yaśovijaya, and Kundakunda, and co‑edited \"Brill’s Encyclopedia of Jainism\" (2020), which has become a key reference work for scholars and students alike.  [[1]](https://www.soas.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-02/Cort%20IJJS%2020%2C%201%202024%20Print%201%20%283%29_0.pdf) [[3]](https://www.soas.ac.uk/about/news/paul-dundas-1952-2023)\n\nWithin institutional and scholarly networks, Dundas served on the Council of the Pali Text Society and was closely associated with the Centre of Jaina Studies (CoJS) at SOAS, University of London, where he acted as an external adviser for many years. In recognition of his contributions to Prakrit and Jain studies, he received the Prakrit Jñānabhāratī International Award in 2019. Colleagues remember him not only for his erudition and breadth of reading across Sanskrit and Prakrit literature, but also for his generosity, humor, and willingness to mentor younger scholars. His death in 2023 was widely felt as a major loss to the field of Jain studies and Indology more broadly.  [[3]](https://www.soas.ac.uk/about/news/paul-dundas-1952-2023) [[2]](https://en.bharatpedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dundas)\n\nFor students of Jainism, Dundas’s work is especially important because it models a careful, source‑grounded approach that respects internal Jain perspectives while maintaining critical academic distance. He consistently foregrounded primary texts—canonical scriptures, commentaries, narrative literature, and sectarian treatises—while situating them within historical processes and social realities. As a result, his writings are frequently recommended as starting points for serious study of Jain philosophy, ethics, ritual, and community life, across both Digambara and Śvetāmbara traditions.",
