  "overview": "Madhyaloka (मध्यलोक) is the central realm of the Jain cosmological universe, positioned between the upper world (Urdhvaloka) and the lower world (Adholoka). It is the only region where embodied beings can attain liberation (moksha), because it contains the human realms in which Tirthankaras are born and where the complete path of right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct can be practiced. Jain cosmology describes Madhyaloka as a vast, disc-shaped expanse with concentric regions, including Jambūdvīpa, Dhātakīkhaṇḍa, and the surrounding oceans and continents.  
\n\nIn both Digambara and Śvetāmbara traditions, Madhyaloka is understood as the realm of mixed pleasure and pain, unlike the upper heavenly realms (which are pleasurable but lack the conditions for liberation) and the lower hellish realms (which are dominated by suffering). Madhyaloka contains humans, animals, and certain classes of celestial and infernal beings who may temporarily visit or manifest within its boundaries.  
\n\nThe most detailed classical descriptions appear in cosmological treatises such as the *Tiloyapaṇṇatti*, *Trilokasāra*, *Jambūdvīpaprajñapti*, and *Saṃgrahaṇī Sūtra*. These texts outline the geography, dimensions, inhabitants, and karmic significance of Madhyaloka. The region is described as having fixed spatial dimensions, with Mount Meru at its center, surrounded by concentric continents and oceans.  
\n\nMadhyaloka’s importance in Jain thought lies not only in its cosmography but also in its soteriological role. It is the only realm where souls can progress through the gunasthānas, attain kevala-jñāna, and ultimately ascend to Siddha-śilā. Thus, Madhyaloka is both a cosmological location and a spiritual opportunity space within Jain doctrine.",
