It is not merely a matter of casual opinion, but of structured, often technically argued divergences about meaning (artha), scope (viṣaya), and authority (prāmāṇya) of scriptural statements. These disagreements arise around issues such as the status of particular āgamas, the intent of key sūtras, the relationship between doctrine and practice, and the reconciliation of apparently conflicting passages.\n\nWithin Jainism, hermeneutic disagreement is shaped by several factors: the early oral transmission of teachings, the loss or partial preservation of some canonical corpora, the emergence of distinct sectarian traditions (Śvetāmbara, Digambara, and sub‑traditions), and the development of sophisticated commentarial cultures. For example, the Śvetāmbara canon, including texts like the Ācārāṅga Sūtra, is accepted as scriptural by Śvetāmbaras but not by Digambaras, who hold that the original oral canon was lost and instead privilege texts such as the Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama and the works of Kundakunda. These debates show how questions of textual history and transmission are inseparable from hermeneutic claims about what the text ‘really’ teaches. Hermeneutic disagreement thus becomes an expected feature of a tradition that emphasizes the complexity of reality and the limitations of any single viewpoint.
