  "overview": "The \"four anuyogas\" (catur-anuyoga) are a traditional Jain way of classifying scriptural and doctrinal material according to its primary mode of exposition. Rather than being four separate philosophies, they are four complementary lenses through which the same path to liberation (mokṣa-mārga) is taught. In Digambara and Śvetāmbara traditions alike, the anuyogas organize vast bodies of literature and practice into intelligible domains, helping students move from narrative inspiration to ethical discipline, cosmological understanding, and finally metaphysical insight.  [[0]](https://insightjainism.vercel.app/docs/Concepts/Anuyog/) [[1]](https://jainworld.com/literature/jain-agams/jain-agam-literature/four-anuyogas/)\n\nMost commonly, the four are named: Prathamanuyoga (or Dharma-kathā-anuyoga), Charan-anuyoga (or Carana-karaṇa-anuyoga), Karan-anuyoga (or Gaṇit-anuyoga), and Dravya-anuyoga (Dravyānuyoga). Prathamanuyoga focuses on religious narratives—lives of Tīrthaṅkaras, Cakravartins, and other exemplary figures—using stories, poetry, and history to cultivate faith (śraddhā), detachment (vairāgya), and moral imagination. Charan-anuyoga gathers texts on conduct and discipline, detailing vows, observances, and the art of living for both householders (śrāvakas) and ascetics. Karan-anuyoga presents cosmology, mathematics, astronomy, and the structure of the universe (loka–aloka), including the workings of karma, the four destinies (gatis), and the complex numerical and spatial schemes that frame Jain cosmography. Dravya-anuyoga expounds philosophical doctrine: the six substances (dravya), the seven or nine tattvas, the nature of soul and non-soul, bondage and liberation, and the logic and epistemology by which right knowledge is established.  [[0]](https://insightjainism.vercel.app/docs/Concepts/Anuyog/) [[1]](https://jainworld.com/literature/jain-agams/jain-agam-literature/four-anuyogas/) [[2]](https://jainknowledge.com/question/9233/english-translations-of-prathamanuyog-karananuyog-charananuyog-and-dravyanuyog)\n\nIn traditional pedagogy, these four are not rigid compartments but mutually reinforcing approaches. Narratives of Prathamanuyoga embody the ethical ideals codified in Charan-anuyoga; those ethical disciplines are grounded in the karmic and cosmological structures explained in Karan-anuyoga; and all three ultimately presuppose and lead toward the metaphysical clarity of Dravya-anuyoga. Pandit Todarmal and later scholars emphasize that proper interpretation of scripture requires awareness of which anuyoga is operative in a given passage, so that one does not confuse metaphorical, conventional descriptions with strict ontological claims. In this way, the four anuyogas are closely linked to the Jain doctrine of multiple viewpoints (naya) and the distinction between conventional (vyavahāra) and ultimate (niścaya) standpoints.  [[3]](https://jaingpt.org/knowledge/shastra_ka_arth_karne_ki_paddhati_aur_char_anuyoga_211996_211996) [[4]](https://jaingpt.org/knowledge/jaindarshan_me_sat_ka_swarup_210983_210983)\n\nAcross sectarian lines, the core thematic content of the four anuyogas remains stable, even though terminology and ordering may vary slightly. Digambara lists often highlight Prathamanuyoga, Charan-anuyoga, Karan-anuyoga, and Dravya-anuyoga, while Śvetāmbara discussions may speak of dharma-kathā, carana-karaṇa, gaṇita, and dravya as interpretive branches of the āgamas. Modern expositions sometimes translate them as narrative, conduct, cosmology/mathematics, and philosophy, respectively. This framework continues to shape contemporary Jain education, from introductory courses to advanced philosophical study, offering a structured way to engage with the breadth of Jain literature while keeping the central aim—realization of the pure self and cessation of karmic bondage—clearly in view.
