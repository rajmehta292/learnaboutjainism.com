  "overview": "In Jain traditions, \"lineage uncertainty\" refers to gaps, ambiguities, or contested claims in the succession of teachers (paramparā) and monastic units (saṅgha, gaṇa, gaccha, kula, śākhā). Lineage is central to how authority is understood in Jainism: ascetic orders, scriptural transmission, ritual practice, and community leadership are all ideally grounded in an unbroken chain reaching back to Mahāvīra and the gaṇadharas. When that chain cannot be clearly reconstructed, or when multiple, conflicting reconstructions exist, questions arise about legitimacy, continuity, and the scope of a given group’s authority.\n\nHistorically, Jain communities have used genealogical lists (paṭṭāvalī), hagiographies, inscriptions, and local memory to preserve monastic successions. These materials were produced in specific social and political contexts—such as the rise and fall of regional polities, migrations of lay communities, and changing patterns of patronage—and they often reflect the needs and perspectives of their compilers. Modern scholarship on Digambara and Śvetāmbara ascetic lineages has shown that seats remembered as continuous successions of high-ranking leaders (like bhaṭṭārakas) sometimes passed through phases of lower-ranking ācāryas or maṇḍalācāryas, and that lineages were relocated, merged, or re-founded in response to external pressures. This makes the historical map of lineages dynamic rather than static, and introduces uncertainty when later narratives present a simplified or idealized picture.\n\nLineage uncertainty appears in several forms. In some cases, there are chronological gaps where no names or dates are securely known, leaving historians to infer continuity from circumstantial evidence. In other cases, different paṭṭāvalīs or sectarian histories give incompatible lists of successors, or assign the same teacher to different branches. There are also situations where monastic and biological lineages intersect—for example, yatis or former ascetics who took wives and had children, whose descendants later claimed both monastic ancestry and caste identity—creating tension about how renunciation, property, and ritual authority are understood. These complexities are further compounded by the major schism between Śvetāmbara and Digambara traditions, each of which preserves its own narrative of how the early community and its lineages developed after Mahāvīra’s nirvāṇa.\n\nFrom a doctrinal perspective, Jainism emphasizes the authority of the Jina’s teaching (jina-vacana) rather than the infallibility of any later teacher. Yet, in practice, the credibility of a given interpretation, discipline, or reform movement is often argued through lineage: who taught whom, under what discipline, and with what authorization. Lineage uncertainty therefore has practical consequences for how communities evaluate new teachings, reforms, or claims to leadership. Modern historians and community scholars respond to this uncertainty by carefully distinguishing between primary scriptural sources, later genealogical compilations, and contemporary reconstructions; by acknowledging where evidence is missing or contradictory; and by applying principles of non-absolutism (anekāntavāda) to avoid treating any single lineage narrative as exhaustive. For lay Jains and students, recognizing lineage uncertainty does not undermine faith in the Jina or the core ethical path, but it does invite a more nuanced understanding of how institutions, titles, and successions have evolved over time.\n\nBecause lineage touches questions of authority, initiation, and community boundaries, unresolved disputes or unclear successions should be approached with caution. Decisions about initiation (dīkṣā), affiliation to a particular gaccha or saṅgha, or acceptance of specific disciplinary rules belong to living lineages and qualified human teachers, not to abstract reconstructions. Where historical or doctrinal questions intersect with personal vows, health, family responsibilities, or legal obligations, consultation with knowledgeable monks, nuns, scholars, and appropriate professionals is essential. An informed awareness of lineage uncertainty can thus coexist with responsible practice: it encourages humility about historical claims, careful attention to sources, and respect for the living communities that continue to interpret and embody the Jain path.",
