  "overview": "Padmanabh Shrivarma Jaini (1923–2021) was one of the most influential modern scholars of Jainism and Buddhism, widely respected for his philological rigor, sect-sensitive analysis, and ability to bridge insider familiarity with academic method. Born into a Digambara Jain family in Nellikar, near Moodabidri in Karnataka, he grew up within a living Jain environment while gradually turning his attention from devotional and communal life to the critical study of texts, doctrines, and practices across South Asian religious traditions. [[0]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmanabh_Jaini)\n\nHis early education at the Digambara Jain Gurukul in Karanja (Vidarbha, Maharashtra) brought him into contact with major twentieth‑century Jain pandits such as Nathuram Premi, Hiralal Jain, and A. N. Upadhye, and later with the Śvetāmbara community through his work in a hostel in Nashik. These experiences shaped his lifelong commitment to treating Digambara and Śvetāmbara traditions with equal seriousness, resisting simplistic binaries and highlighting the internal diversity of Jain thought. After completing a B.A. (Honours) in Sanskrit and Prakrit and an M.A. in Sanskrit, he pursued advanced study of Pali and Theravāda Buddhism in Sri Lanka, earning the Tipiṭakācārya degree and deepening his expertise in canonical Buddhist literature. [[1]](https://www.bhu.ac.in/Site/Page/1_106_638_6333_Department-of-Pali-And-Buddhist-Studies-Retired-Teachers) [[2]](https://digjainwiki.org/wiki/shri-padmnabh-jain/)\n\nJaini’s academic career took him from Banaras Hindu University to the School of Oriental and African Studies (SOAS) in London, the University of Michigan at Ann Arbor, and finally the University of California, Berkeley, where he served as Professor of Buddhist Studies and co‑founded the Group in Buddhist Studies. At Berkeley he became a central figure in the institutional development of Buddhist and Jain studies, mentoring generations of students and continuing to publish critical editions, translations, and synthetic works even after his formal retirement in 1994. His colleagues and students remember him not only for his scholarship but also for his disciplined lifestyle, daily walks, and sustained engagement with younger researchers, including the establishment of the Padmanabh S. Jaini Graduate Student Award in Buddhist Studies. [[3]](https://buddhiststudies.berkeley.edu/people/padmanabh-jaini) [[4]](https://jainismo.org/jaini/)\n\nIn Jain studies, Jaini’s most widely known book is \"The Jaina Path of Purification\" (1979), a comprehensive, tradition‑aware introduction to Jain doctrine, ethics, ritual, and community life. The work is notable for its balanced treatment of Śvetāmbara and Digambara sources, careful use of canonical and commentarial literature, and attention to contemporary practice. It has become a standard reference for students and scholars, often serving as a bridge between primary texts and modern academic discourse. His \"Gender and Salvation: Jaina Debates on the Spiritual Liberation of Women\" (1991) offers a detailed study of sectarian arguments about women’s capacity for liberation, showing how doctrinal positions are intertwined with social and institutional realities. [[4]](https://jainismo.org/jaini/) [[2]](https://digjainwiki.org/wiki/shri-padmnabh-jain/)\n\nBeyond Jainism, Jaini made major contributions to Buddhist studies, particularly in the field of Abhidharma. His critical edition of the \"Abhidharmadīpa\" and related works, as well as his studies of Pali texts such as the \"Paññāsa‑Jātaka\" and \"Subodhālaṅkāra\", exemplify his meticulous approach to manuscript comparison, textual history, and doctrinal analysis. These editions and monographs are frequently cited as foundational for the study of early Buddhist scholasticism and narrative literature. His collected essays on both Jainism and Buddhism—\"Collected Papers on Jaina Studies\" (2000) and \"Collected Papers on Buddhist Studies\" (2001)—gather decades of research, ranging from technical doctrinal issues to broader reflections on interreligious dialogue and the comparative study of liberation. [[1]](https://www.bhu.ac.in/Site/Page/1_106_638_6333_Department-of-Pali-And-Buddhist-Studies-Retired-Teachers) [[4]](https://jainismo.org/jaini/)\n\nJaini’s work is especially important for modern Jain communities and students of Jainism because it models a way of engaging with the tradition that is both deeply respectful and critically reflective. He consistently distinguished between primary textual evidence, later commentary, and contemporary interpretation, and he was attentive to the ways in which sectarian identities, gender, and historical context shape doctrinal positions. While he never claimed religious authority as an Acharya or monk, his writings have significantly influenced how Jainism is presented in universities, encyclopedias, and interreligious forums. His legacy continues through his publications, his students, and the ongoing use of his works in both academic and community settings.",
