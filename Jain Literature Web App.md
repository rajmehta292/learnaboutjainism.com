This means: - No manipulative urgency - No guilt-based language - No fear-based religious messaging - No shaming users for not practicing enough - No aggressive pop-ups - No autoplaying sound - No distracting motion - No engagement traps - No streak pressure - No competitive spiritual rankings - No language suggesting that casual learners are spiritually inferior - No dark patterns - No unnecessary collection of personal data The interface should reduce pressure rather than create it. ## Anekantavada in Information Design The site should make room for multiple viewpoints without becoming vague. This means: - Clearly identifying tradition-specific teachings - Showing differences between editions - Presenting multiple translations - Distinguishing sacred tradition from historical scholarship - Allowing different Jain communities to describe their own practices - Avoiding phrases such as “Jains believe” when beliefs vary - Showing disagreement without creating conflict - Teaching the user how context changes interpretation Anekantavada should be visible in the structure of information, not merely described on one philosophy page. ## Aparigraha in Visual Design Aparigraha should appear as restraint. This means: - Fewer decorative elements - Fewer competing colors - Fewer interface controls - Less visual noise - No unnecessary animation - No oversized promotional statistics - No endless badges - No excessive card effects - No decorative elements that do not help learning The site should contain abundant knowledge without looking crowded. ## Satya in Editorial Practice Truthfulness means: - Every major claim is sourced - Uncertainty is visible - Missing information remains missing - Tradition is not confused with historical evidence - Summaries are not presented as quotations - AI output is not presented as scripture - OCR is not presented as verified transcription - The site does not claim completeness it has not achieved ## Samayika in Interaction Design The interface should support focused attention. Reading a text, studying a concept, or comparing traditions should feel stable and uninterrupted. The interface should not constantly invite the visitor to click elsewhere. ## Svadhyaya in Learning Structure The site should encourage study through: - Progressive explanations - Primary-source reading - Reflection - Cross-references - Glossary assistance - Learning paths - Questions for continued study The goal is not simply to deliver answers. It is to help visitors develop an informed and independent relationship with Jain knowledge. --- # 4. The Architectural Metaphor Use the experience of sacred architecture as a structural metaphor without turning the website into a literal digital temple. ## The Threshold The homepage is the threshold. It should be calm and simple. It should not immediately expose every dataset, feature, number, and section. Its purpose is to orient the visitor and help them choose a direction. ## The Courtyard Whitespace functions as the courtyard. It creates breathing room between ideas and allows visitors to pause before moving deeper. Whitespace is not unused space. It is part of the experience. ## The Mandapa Topic hubs function as gathering spaces. Examples: - Foundations - Texts - Philosophy - Practice - History - Traditions - People and places Each hub should organize related material clearly before the visitor enters individual pages. ## The Shrine The primary subject of a page should have one clear focal point. A scripture page should focus on the scripture. A Tirthankara page should focus on that Tirthankara. A practice page should focus on the meaning and context of that practice. Do not surround every subject with competing calls to action. ## The Bhandar The full-text library and manuscript catalog function as the bhandar: the protected repository of textual knowledge. This part of the platform should feel precise, stable, bibliographic, and trustworthy. ## The Path Learning paths connect the spaces. The site should always help the visitor understand: - Where they are - What they are studying - What may be useful to learn first - What they can study next - How deeply they are currently reading --- # 5. Motion Language ## Motion Principle **Stillness is the default. Motion exists only to clarify change.** The website should not constantly move. Animation should feel like: - A page settling into place - Light entering a courtyard - A door opening quietly - A line being drawn - A manuscript page turning - A breath slowing - A subtle change in attention It should not feel like: - A product launch - A gaming interface - A social-media app - A futuristic AI dashboard - A cinematic trailer ## Motion Categories ### Arrival Used when a page or major section first appears. Recommended behavior: - Fade from 0 to 1 - Move upward by no more than 6–10 px - Duration: 320–480 ms - No large staggered card cascade ### Attention Used when a user selects, bookmarks, or opens something. Recommended behavior: - Small tonal change - Fine underline movement - Soft border emphasis - Scale no larger than approximately 1.01–1.02 - Duration: 140–220 ms ### Continuity Used for expanding sections, changing reading views, or moving between passages. Recommended behavior: - Gentle crossfade - Controlled height transition - Preserve scroll position - Duration: 180–300 ms ### Completion Used for actions such as saving a bookmark or copying a citation. Recommended behavior: - Brief checkmark - Short text confirmation - No fireworks - No confetti - No celebratory sound ## Motion Timing - Immediate feedback: 90–140 ms - Standard control transition: 160–240 ms - Content reveal: 300–480 ms - Rare ceremonial transition: up to 600 ms Avoid transitions longer than approximately 700 ms. ## Recommended Easing Use a soft deceleration curve such as: `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.2, 1)` Avoid: - Elastic movement - Bounce easing - Spring overshoot - Constant floating - Repeated pulsing - Rotating icons - Typewriter effects - Particle systems - Sparkle effects ## Scroll Animation Do not animate every card as the visitor scrolls. Writing Principles ## Begin With Meaning Explain what a subject means before listing rules, dates, classifications, or subtypes. ## Provide Context Before Detail Before explaining twelve austerities, explain why Jain traditions discuss austerity. Before listing the fourteen gunasthanas, explain what the model is intended to describe. ## Layer the Information Every important page should offer several depths. ### In brief A concise explanation for a new learner. ### Learn A fuller beginner-friendly lesson. ### Study More detailed terminology, structure, and doctrinal relationships. ### Sources Primary texts, editions, translations, citations, and scholarly notes. Do not force every visitor to read the most technical explanation first. ## Explain Terms on First Use Example: > Samvara, the stopping of new karmic influx, is one of the central stages in the Jain account of liberation. Do not write: > Samvara leads to nirjara through gupti, samiti, dharma, anupreksha, parishahajaya, and charitra. without first orienting the learner. ## Attribute Claims Use: - Jain texts describe… - In Digambara traditions… - In many Svetambara communities… - This commentary interprets… - The traditional account states… - Modern scholars generally date… - The surviving edition contains… - Practices vary by community and teacher… Avoid: - Jainism says… - All Jains believe… - It is scientifically proven… - This definitely happened… - The scripture clearly proves… unless the evidence genuinely supports such certainty. --- # 18. Presentation of Practices This is one of the most important parts of the brand. Visitors should not read about fasting, vows, meditation, monastic conduct, or dietary care and conclude: - Jainism is only for ascetics - They must perform everything immediately - Ordinary life is spiritually meaningless - They are failing if they cannot follow an advanced practice - Jain practice is primarily restriction - Spiritual development is a competition - Health risks should be ignored - Every Jain follows the same discipline ## Practice Introduction Every practice section should begin with language similar to: > Jain practice ranges from small daily acts of awareness to demanding forms of monastic discipline. These practices are not all expected of every person. This page explains their purpose, context, traditional forms, and the different ways people may encounter them. ## Practice Page Structure ### What it is A simple definition. ### Why it matters Its ethical, devotional, or spiritual purpose. ### Who practices it Clarify whether it is: - Common among householders - Observed on particular days - Undertaken with guidance - Primarily monastic - Tradition-specific - Optional - Advanced ### Ways people begin Offer modest, non-prescriptive entry points where appropriate. ### Traditional forms Explain the fuller discipline without hiding its difficulty. ### Differences among traditions Show relevant variation. ### Source foundations Identify texts and commentaries. ### Health or safety context Include only when relevant, clearly separated from religious teaching. ## Practice Spectrum Use a visible spectrum rather than one intimidating instruction list. ### Understand Learn the meaning of the practice. ### Observe Watch, listen, attend, or read about the practice. ### Begin gently Explore a modest form appropriate to the learner’s circumstances. ### Traditional lay observance Describe established householder forms. ### Monastic or advanced discipline Explain the most demanding form in its proper context. This structure makes the full tradition visible without implying that the visitor must immediately undertake its most difficult form. ## Preferred Language Use: - Some practitioners… - A person may begin by… - In its traditional lay form… - Monastics observe a more extensive form… - The appropriate form depends on health, circumstance, tradition, and guidance. - Learning about a practice does not require undertaking it. - Many people first encounter this practice through… - The practice is understood as… - Communities differ in how this is observed. Avoid: - Every serious Jain must… - Never break the vow under any circumstance. - This is the most powerful method. - Beginners should prove their discipline by… - The practice guarantees… - Failure creates… - Only advanced souls can… - This will purify your body. - This destroys all karma immediately. ## Fasting Lead with purpose rather than deprivation. Preferred opening: > Fasting is one expression of tapa, or disciplined spiritual effort. Jain traditions include many forms of tapa, and not all involve complete abstention from food or water. Practices vary greatly in intensity and are shaped by health, age, community, tradition, and spiritual guidance. Do not present fasting as: - A challenge - A level - A badge of purity - A competitive achievement - A wellness technique - A guaranteed method of karmic destruction - A measure of someone’s devotion ## Monastic Practice Explain monastic disciplines with reverence and context. Do not turn them into generalized instructions for the public. Use: > This is a monastic discipline undertaken within a formal religious commitment and under the guidance of an established community. ## Sallekhana Sallekhana requires exceptionally careful presentation. It should never appear: - In a difficulty ranking - As a challenge - As a self-guided practice - Alongside ordinary diet options - As an aspirational achievement - As “fasting unto death” without careful doctrinal, ethical, legal, historical, and health context It should have a dedicated, source-based page with clear tradition notes and responsible language. --- # 19. Presentation of Philosophy Philosophy should feel approachable without being reduced to slogans. ## Recommended Structure ### One-sentence understanding A clear starting point. ### The central idea A beginner-friendly explanation. ### Why it matters How the concept connects to the path of liberation or Jain ethics. ### Important terms Definitions with pronunciation and aliases. ### Relationships Connections to other Jain concepts. ### Across traditions Differences in interpretation or emphasis. ### Read the sources Primary passages and commentaries. ### Questions for reflection Open-ended questions, not doctrinal tests. ## Avoid False Scientific Framing Do not call Jain philosophical systems “scientific” merely to make them appear modern or credible. Instead of: > Karma is a precise scientific law. Use: > Jain philosophy describes karma as a subtle form of matter that becomes associated with the soul through activity and passion. Presentation of Traditions Begin with shared foundations before differences.
